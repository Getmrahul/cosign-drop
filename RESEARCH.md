# Cosign drop: research and working brief

Researched September 29, 2026. This is preparation for choosing a drop; no concept has been selected or implemented.

**What Dhruv actually asked for**

[Hiring challenge](https://x.com/droovg/status/2103640223972557191), edited September 26:

- Look at the MTS drop archive.
- Build a drop, preferably about Cosign or social networks.
- Post about it publicly.
- DM Dhruv the Git repository and a transcript of the agent conversation used to build it.
- Demonstrate creativity, decisiveness, speed, and empathy.
- He promises at least a response, and says everyone who posts something will be let into Cosign.

No deadline, prescribed stack, required API integration, or scoring rubric is stated in that post. A response and admission are not an employment guarantee. The repository and agent transcript are explicit deliverables, so preserve the development conversation from the beginning. Publication and messaging remain future actions, not part of this research request.

[Dhruv’s clarification](https://x.com/droovg/status/2103669927140032706) rejects a narrow definition of a drop as a scrolling write-up with interactive components. Drops can take any form. His early examples included interactive explanations and visualizations of unstructured data; the essential requirement is a theme represented in a new way.

**Working definition**

A drop is a self-contained digital experience built around an idea, question, story, or body of evidence. Its form should make the subject understandable or explorable in a distinctive way. This is my synthesis of the clarification and examples, not an additional rule from Dhruv.

Interactivity can be a calculator, a map, an annotated archive, a staged explanation, a simulation, a gallery, or a useful small tool. The archive does not establish one mandatory visual style, navigation pattern, or level of technical complexity.

**Cosign: what it is**

Cosign is a curated professional network for the startup ecosystem. Erik’s [launch post](https://x.com/eriktorenberg/status/2103493671866060958) describes a directory of people and companies enriched with work history, relationships, endorsements, discovery and professional intent. Admission is through being cosigned or applying. He presents it as a community rather than a standalone business; the [official disclosure](https://cosign.co/disclosure) confirms a16z ownership.

The underlying thesis is that professional belief already travels through introductions, public praise, investments and shared work, but much of that context is scattered or temporary. Cosign aims to preserve it and make it discoverable. The [launch essay](https://www.a16z.news/p/how-silicon-valley-knows-its-people) also emphasizes the reciprocal benefit: someone promising gets noticed, and the early believer earns a reputation for judgment. This is the authors’ product thesis, not proof that the system will deliver those outcomes.

**How its pieces fit together**

| Layer | What it does | Evidence/status |
|---|---|---|
| People and company profiles | Collect professional context and attributed endorsements around an entity. | Described in the launch and landing page. Only a limited public profile was accessible in this session. |
| Cosigns | Express specific kinds of belief and experience. | The official discussion names career-shaping people, people one would work with anywhere, and people to watch. |
| Curated lists | Publish someone’s judgment about people or companies worth paying attention to. | Directly inspected on the public Lists page. |
| Community questions | Gather recommendations around a specific question and give the nominated people persistent visibility. | Described in the official discussion and landing page. Not exercised inside the gated product. |
| Announcements / Industry Watch | Collect company and career updates and connect relevant praise back to profiles. | Described by the founders and advertised on the landing page. |
| Jobs | Connect discovery of trusted companies to open roles. | Public Jobs page inspected, including list filters, job details, external applications and the Signal control. |
| Private intent | Express interest in a person, employer or future collaboration. | The job-interest control is visible today. The founders describe broader mutual-interest introductions; their full availability was not verified. |

The [official a16z discussion](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-ywZlfznTa4) supplies additional operating detail. At roughly 4:12–6:18 it explains the three core cosign categories. At 12:52–18:35 it discusses private intent and lists. At 18:37–22:26 it explains how questions and announcements can build lasting reputation. At 43:15–46:04 it describes assembling profiles from public information with AI and accepting corrections or removal requests. I read the exported automatic captions; names and wording can contain transcription errors.

The discussion presents Cosign as deliberately positive and gives particular attention to people whose contributions are well known to teammates but not publicly visible. It also describes publicly sourced profile construction as a way to address the empty-network problem at launch.

**What I could directly verify in the product**

- Visiting Cosign used the existing browser session and showed a waitlist page requiring administrator approval. No account was created during this research.
- [Lists](https://cosign.co/lists) remained accessible. There are Curated and Community tabs, list search, filters, sorting, curator attribution and related lists.
- [Packy’s Top Companies](https://cosign.co/lists/packym/top-companies) displayed 36 companies with descriptions, founder links, occasional curator commentary, and a route to jobs at those companies.
- Lists can be shared on X or downloaded as an image. These controls were observed, not used.
- The public [Packy profile](https://cosign.co/packy) showed a biography and role, then an authentication restriction. Discover returned to the waitlist.
- [Jobs](https://cosign.co/jobs) displayed open roles, curated-company-list filters, job details, save controls, external Apply links and a Signal button. Its tooltip says the signal privately informs the company of interest and is not a job application. No interest was sent and no application was made.
- The [privacy policy](https://cosign.co/privacy-policy) describes profiles, questions/answers, cosigns, lists and possible sharing of interest signals at the user’s direction. It does not establish that all signals are hidden until mutual interest. Do not promise that without verifying the specific flow.

The [public landing page](https://cosign.co/) advertises profile discovery, curator reputation, community picks, Industry Watch and jobs. Its illustrative profile counts and salary cards should not be treated as measured usage or proof of a particular ranking algorithm.

**Implications for our eventual drop — interpretation, not requirements**

The most relevant subjects are the mechanisms behind trust, attribution, early belief, shared work and opportunity. A strong drop could make one of those mechanisms visible through real evidence or an explicitly labeled model.

The strongest pattern across the examples is a specific question paired with a suitable interaction. Choose the question first. A graph is useful only if following an edge reveals something meaningful; an animation is useful when it explains change or causality; a dataset is useful when the viewer can discover something in it.

The challenge’s four stated qualities translate into practical choices: originality in the framing, a firm scope, a complete and responsive experience, and care for the reader’s comprehension. These are inferred design criteria, not a published scoring system.

Potential subject areas to consider later include how one early endorsement opens a path, how a person’s contribution survives a company move, how different relationship types carry different information, and how an early believer builds a track record. None is yet a selected concept.

Keep source attribution, relationship context and timing visible when using real people. Distinguish an observed endorsement from an inferred association, and a simulation from a real career outcome. We have not established access to Cosign’s internal graph, API, scoring, or complete member experience.
